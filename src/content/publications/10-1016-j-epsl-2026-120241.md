summaryEn: "Decadal GNSS time series resolve a transient component lasting about two years and a steady component persisting through 2022. Three-dimensional viscoelastic models indicate that back-arc mantle viscosity is up to two orders of magnitude lower than continental asthenosphere viscosity, controlling the early transient response while the continental asthenosphere controls the long-term deformation."

researchQuestion: "How does viscosity heterogeneity beneath the East Sea and Korean Peninsula control far-field postseismic deformation after the 2011 Tohoku-Oki earthquake?"
dataAndRegion: "Decadal GNSS displacement time series across the Korean Peninsula and a regional model spanning the Korean Peninsula, East Sea, and Japanese subduction system were analyzed."
methods: "Geodetic inversion was combined with three-dimensional spherical viscoelastic simulations that represent distinct continental asthenosphere and back-arc mantle viscosities."
mainFindings: "Low-viscosity back-arc mantle controls the early transient deformation, whereas the continental asthenosphere governs the steady-state response across the Korean Peninsula."
quantitativeEvidence: "The transient lasts about 2 years; continental asthenosphere viscosity is 10^18–10^19 Pa s, and back-arc mantle viscosity is up to two orders of magnitude lower."
limitations: "The inferred viscosities depend on the adopted three-dimensional mantle geometry and rheological parameterization used to explain the GNSS displacements."
