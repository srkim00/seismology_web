Optional details: exact two-layer calculation

k = (ρ₂ − ρ₁) / (ρ₂ + ρ₁)
ρₐ = ρ₁ {1 + 4a Σn=1…∞ kⁿ [1/√(a² + 4n²h²) − 1/√(4a² + 4n²h²)]}

This image-source series is the forward response of a Wenner array on a flat, isotropic, two-layer Earth. The lower layer extends indefinitely downward. The displayed curve is computed from this expression, not interpolated between end values.

As a/h approaches zero, ρₐ approaches ρ₁. As a/h becomes very large, ρₐ approaches ρ₂. A finite plot range may not reach either limit, especially for strong contrasts.

Within the controls, |k| is at most 0.9802. Summation stops when a conservative bound on the remaining dimensionless series contribution is less than 10⁻¹⁰. The surface must be approximately flat, the layer boundary horizontal, and the array far from lateral changes.

This demonstration predicts data from a known model. Inferring an unknown model from field data is an inverse problem. Noise, finite spacing range, and unmodelled layers can allow different models to explain similar data.

Theory: Lowrie, Fundamentals of Geophysics, Chapter 4, §4.3 (pp. 252–276), resistivity sounding; UBC GeoSci: DC interpretation.
