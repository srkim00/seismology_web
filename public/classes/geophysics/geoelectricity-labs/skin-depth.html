Amplitude remaining at depth

Skin depth is an attenuation scale. It is the depth where amplitude falls to 1/e ≈ 37%. It is neither a sharp cutoff nor a guaranteed investigation depth.

1. Predict

Increase frequency by a factor of 100. Will skin depth become 100 times smaller, or 10 times smaller?

2. Try

At 1 Hz, change resistivity from 100 Ω m to 1 Ω m. Compare the skin depths.

3. Explain

Why do MT studies of deep Earth structure use long-period signals? Why can a conductive shallow layer obscure deeper structure?

Optional details: equation, assumptions, and limits

δ = √[ρ / (πfμ₀)] ≈ 503 √(ρ/f) m
A(z) / A(0) = exp(−z/δ)
δ(10f) = δ(f) / √10

ρ is in Ω m and f in Hz. μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m. The graph shows an amplitude envelope, not the instantaneous oscillating field or its phase. The horizontal depth axis is logarithmic; every equal step multiplies depth by ten.

Assumptions: a vertically propagating harmonic plane wave in a homogeneous, isotropic, non-magnetic good conductor, with conduction current much greater than displacement current. The model neglects layering, local sources, and topography. The radio/radar regime may require the full wave expression.

Real depth sensitivity also depends on source geometry, signal-to-noise ratio, survey design, and conductivity structure. A depth of 2δ retains about 14% amplitude and 3δ about 5%. A field remains nonzero below δ.

Theory: Lowrie, Fundamentals of Geophysics, Chapter 4, §4.3 (pp. 252–276); EM GeoSci: Skin depth.
